How it works

These pages explain how devvm does what the other sections describe, for readers who want to check a claim, such as security reviewers and agents. They name the files devvm ships and the messages it prints. You don’t need them to use devvm.

  • Overview: what devvm does, step by step, when it makes, opens, stops and deletes a VM, and the state files it keeps.
  • Installing and updating: what the install script downloads and checks, devvm doctor --json, devvm’s own files, and upgrading.
  • Images and caching: how images and shared tools are built, keyed and cached, and how devvm checks what they start from.
  • Port forwarding: how devvm’s forwarder forwards a VM’s ports to your machine.
  • The desktop: how devvm desktop serves a VM’s desktop on your machine, checks its password and keeps your clipboard out, and why devvm doesn’t forward X11.
  • Network tiers: each tier’s confinement, the proxy, the self-check, and how secrets get into requests.
  • .git protection: what devvm locks, when it checks the locks, and what they can’t stop.
  • Security model: what devvm protects on your machine and how, and what it doesn’t protect.
  • Backends: each backend’s features, native or emulated, and what happens when one is missing.
  • Lima: devvm’s Lima home, confinement, clones and port forwarding on Lima.
  • Design notes: why devvm is built this way, what failed, and what’s still open.
